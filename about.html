I wanted to include something more personal on my about page. These are my thoughts on resilience, an essay I used for my college applications.

Forged Resilience

A person’s success in life is built on resilience. The challenges I experienced tested and strengthened my determination and grit. Growing up with a father whose emotional detachment and anger created constant conflict, surviving a war that occupied my town, and adapting to life as a refugee in a foreign country each demanded that I mature quickly and take responsibility for myself. Those moments shaped the foundation of my character and prepared me to confront difficulties with confidence and perseverance. My ability to endure hardship and grow from it demonstrates the resilience that defines who I am.

My father’s emotional detachment and anger forced me to develop the original foundation of my resilience. His inability to remain objective in disagreements led to frequent arguments between my parents. When I was old enough to form my own opinions, I joined these discussions. My father had little respect for anyone unless it benefited him, and arguing with him often felt futile. From that, I learned who I did not want to become. I intentionally practiced patience and understanding with others, even when it felt unnatural, because I refused to repeat his mistakes. I survived my childhood with him and eventually left, determined to create a life built on empathy and responsibility.

When the Russo-Ukrainian war began, my town was invaded at 4 a.m., and Russian forces occupied it for a month. Communication, travel, electricity, running water, and access to supplies were cut off, testing everyone’s integrity, courage, and sense of right and wrong. Marauders thrived, making the streets dangerous. Despite being in eighth grade, I remained calm. I helped my family solve communication problems by building a small battery-powered radio and provided neighbors with water from our well. The resilience I had already built carried me through; without that foundation, the war might have broken me. Instead, it confirmed my strength and taught me that I could adapt and grow under extreme circumstances.

In August 2022, my mother and I fled Ukraine and arrived in the United States as refugees, leaving our entire lives behind. I had to reinvent myself in a world where every detail of my past life was foreign: a new language, a new culture, a new education system, and a new identity. Adapting to this new life felt like rewiring my brain. Everything was uncertain–I was not even sure if I had a future here. On top of that, I was completing high school diploma programs for both Ukraine and the United States while navigating a completely unfamiliar world and forming a new identity. Amid these challenges, I found ways to thrive. I started the Garden Club, which collaborates with a local food pantry. Before, the pantry only had canned goods and packaged items, but our club now grows and donates fresh vegetables. Back in Ukraine, I never had a community aligned with my interests or curiosity for history. Here, I founded the History Club, which holds three types of meetings: lectures, debates, and project days, where members research and present on topics that interest them, creating posters, infographics, or presentations. Through these initiatives, I carved out a space where I belong, pursued my passions, and made an impact despite starting over in a new country. Immigration was not just relocation; it was transformation.

By enduring my father’s volatility, surviving a war, and adapting to life in a new country, I cultivated resilience that allows me to face adversity and grow from it. Those defining chapters taught me to withstand pressure, assume responsibility, and thrive even when the ground beneath me shifts. The strength and perseverance I developed became my foundation, guiding me in every challenge I encounter and shaping me into the determined, adaptable person I am today.
